features

how to play

features is a daily untangle puzzle: move the dots until no lines cross

each day brings six small boards, the same for everyone. a board is a network of dots, which the game calls neurons, joined by lines, and some of the lines cross. drag the neurons until no two lines cross, and the board is untangled. that is the whole game; the rest of this page is detail.

the board

the lines come in colours, and each colour is a feature: a group of neurons and the lines between them. every crossing is a conflict, and the head of the board counts them. when a feature has no conflicts left it is freed, and it settles into a stamp, a small picture such as a house, a cup or a leaf, which joins your collection. there are 100 to find.

special neurons

boards 1 and 2 are plain. later boards mix in four kinds of special neuron, in a different combination each day.

  • frozen: filled in its feature’s colour. it stays where it is, so move the others around it.
  • fused: a ring of two halves, one in each of two features’ colours. it belongs to both until you tap it to split it, and then each half moves on its own. neither feature can be freed until it is split.
  • once: a ring with a small dot in its centre. it moves one time only, then fills in and stays put until its feature is freed.
  • linked: two neurons on different features, joined by a dotted line. drag either and both move together, by the same amount, for one turn. the dotted line never crosses anything.

turns and grades

every drag is a turn. a split is free, and so is a neuron carried back to where it started. each board is graded by the turns it took: perfect in the fewest turns it can be cleared in, sharp in one more, clean in two more, and solved in any more than that. no board can be failed: it plays on until it is untangled, and the head of the board counts the turns left to the next grade.

a day of six

the six open at midnight where you are, and each is harder than the one before, up to the sixth. today’s six are free to play in any browser, on a phone or a computer, with no download and no account. a free account also opens yesterday’s set, and one unlock opens every day before that. the menu holds five short lessons for practice.

tips

  • the count of conflicts changes while you hold a neuron, so you can try a spot before you let go.
  • carry a neuron back to the faint ring where you picked it up and the move costs nothing.
  • a freed feature stops counting, so the space it took opens up for the rest.
  • the move that clears the most crossings now is not always the one that leads to perfect.
  • on a keyboard, tab to a neuron and move it with the arrow keys.

where the idea comes from

in mathematics, a network that can be drawn with no lines crossing is called planar. untangling one by moving its dots is an old puzzle: planarity, a flash game made by john tantalo in 2005, made it popular online. every features board is made and checked before it ships, so each one can be untangled, and perfect is the fewest turns that takes.
